Dear Editor,

We would like to submit a research paper entitled ``Do Language Models Write Away Their Own Oversight? A Preregistered Test of Constitutional Self-Revision'' for consideration for publication in \textit{Machine Learning}.

Our paper studies the phenomenon of LLMs reflecting upon, critiquing and refining the principles they operate under -- in contexts as varied as constitutionally-trained LLMs and self-refining agents. In doing so, we put to an empirical test one argument put forward in AI safety research, namely that as such reflection becomes recursive and models revise their constitutions based on their critiques of these, they will progressively write away commitments to defer to human oversight. Contrary to expectations, in our empirical instantiation, we find no evidence of selective erosion for oversight commitments, but do find evidence of this for stylistic self-restraints.

We think that our work would be a good fit for your journal for three reasons. First, the paper contributes an empirical approach to studying value change under self-revision -- using matched item categories, control conditions and discrete-time survival analysis. Second, we transparently report a preregistered null result and share an honest reflection on where our evidentiary base is more and less solid. Third, we make our data, decision log and (frozen) analysis code public such that our analyses are easy to reproduce and our findings easy to build upon.

The work was preregistered at OSF: \url{https://osf.io/mw86p}. This manuscript and its content have not been published or submitted for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission of this work. No previously published figures, tables or text are reused in any way. We have adhered to the instructions given to authors for the preparation of a manuscript for submission, and the Contribution Information Sheet for \textit{Machine Learning} is enclosed herewith.

Thank you for considering our work.
