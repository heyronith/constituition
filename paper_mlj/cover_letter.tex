Dear Editor,

We submit ``Do Language Models Write Away Their Own Oversight? A Preregistered Test of Constitutional Self-Revision'' for consideration as a research article in \textit{Machine Learning}.

Language models are increasingly used to critique and revise the principles that govern them, from constitutional training to self-refining agents. A long-standing argument in AI safety predicts that such reflection should gradually remove commitments to human oversight. We test this prediction directly and find that it does not hold in our setting: oversight commitments were not selectively eroded, whereas stylistic self-restraints were.

We believe the paper suits \textit{Machine Learning} for three reasons. First, it contributes an empirical method for measuring value drift under iterated self-revision, combining matched item categories, control conditions and discrete-time survival analysis. Second, it reports a preregistered null result together with a frank account of where the evidence is fragile. Third, it releases the full dataset, the frozen analysis code and the decision log, so that every result can be checked and extended.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. The study was preregistered on OSF (\url{https://osf.io/mw86p}). No previously published figures, tables or text are reused. The \textit{Machine Learning} Contribution Information Sheet is enclosed.

Thank you for considering our work.
