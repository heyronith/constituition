**Working title:** "Do Language Models Write Away Their Own Oversight? A Preregistered Test of Constitutional Self-Revision"

---

## Abstract (~200 words, one paragraph)
- **Setup:** the claim that corrigibility is not reflectively stable, made testable with LMs revising their own principles.
- **Design:** 7 configs; 35-principle constitution; 20 stateless rounds, one change per round; matched categories (oversight vs agentic vs stylistic); 4 conditions; preregistered.
- **Measurement:** LLM judge plus a second judge (α `\Alpha`); `\Nevents` events over `\Nitemrounds` item-rounds.
- **Result 1:** no selective erosion. HR `\HoneHR` [`\HoneLo`, `\HoneHi`], but item-dependent: item-aware CIs include 1.
- **Result 2:** stylistic restraints erode most under reflection (`\SelfErodedSR` vs `\SelfErodedNE` under neutral edits).
- **Result 3:** no self-specificity; no behavioural effect, with positive controls validated.
- **Close:** release statement.

## 1. Introduction (5–6 paragraphs)
1. **Hook.** Models increasingly critique and rewrite the principles that govern them (constitutional training, self-refinement, agents editing their own memory and instructions). Iterating such revision compounds small changes.
2. **The worry.** The classical argument (Soares et al. 2015): a reflecting agent may discard constraints on itself, especially those enabling shutdown and modification. If so, oversight could be "written away" with no adversary.
3. **The gap.**
   - Prior work measures oversight *behaviour*: shutdown resistance; alignment faking; scheming.
   - Self-evolving-agent work shows erosion under *task or reward pressure*: SEABench; Alignment Tipping.
   - Nobody has isolated *unpressured reflection* on written values with matched controls.
   - Concurrent GitHub pipelines have no controls and no analysis.
4. **Our approach (one paragraph).** Iterated-learning chains as a probe of each model's "reflective prior". Matched categories; self vs other framing; paraphrase and neutral controls; survival analysis; two judges; behavioural follow-through; preregistered with frozen code.
5. **Findings (one paragraph).** The three results above, stated plainly, with the item-level caveat.
6. **Contributions (bulleted, 4–5).** Paradigm and dataset; a null on selectivity; stylistic drift driven by reflection; a validated null behavioural effect; exploratory moderators.

## 2. Related work (4 short paragraphs)
1. **Constitutions and principles:** CAI, Collective CAI, specific vs general principles, constitution adherence. All are human-authored; none is iteratively self-revised.
2. **Iterated transmission and self-refinement:** telephone-game attractors; broken telephone; Markovian chains; convergence to self. We use attractors as a measurement tool.

## 5. Discussion (4 paragraphs)
1. **What the results mean.** Under unpressured reflection, these models don't write away oversight. Drift targets constraints they plausibly see as arbitrary.
2. **Channel matters.** Contrast with pressure, reward and adversarial channels in prior work.
3. **Reflective priors and training.** Post-training may instil oversight-protective priors; the moderator evidence is exploratory.
4. **Limitations (bulleted is fine):**
   - 5 items per category;
   - open models ≤ 31B;
   - forced single edits;
   - text vs disposition;
   - AI judges, no human coding;
   - censoring in two configs;
   - stateless chains, no reward or memory;
   - vignette battery.

**Future work:** more items; frontier models; task pressure; persistent memory; training on revised constitutions.
