\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

% P1: constitutions and principles.
\paragraph{Constitutions and principles.}
Constitutional AI trains models against a written list of principles using model-generated critiques and revisions \citep{bai2022constitutional}. Later work studies the choice of principles \citep{kundu2023specific}, sources them from the public \citep{huang2024collective}, and audits how well models adhere to them \citep{jakkli2026constitutions}. In all of this work the principles are authored by people. We instead let the governed model iteratively rewrite its own principles and measure what survives.

% P2: iterated transmission and self-refinement.
\paragraph{Iterated transmission and self-refinement.}
Passing text repeatedly through language models produces systematic drift toward model-specific attractors \citep{perez2024telephone,mohamed2025broken,geng2026markovian}. Recursive self-refinement converges to model-preferred fixed points, with most change in early rounds \citep{wu2026converge}. We use this convergence as a measurement tool. Paraphrase and neutral-edit conditions, together with matched content categories, separate value-specific erosion from generic textual drift.

% P3: self-evolving agents and value drift.
\paragraph{Self-evolving agents and value drift.}
Agents that rewrite their own controllers, memories or skills under task pressure show eroded safety behaviour \citep{das2026seabench}. Reward-driven self-evolution can tip agents away from alignment \citep{han2025alignmenttipping}. Misaligned goals written into persistent memory propagate to later agents \citep{das2026selfpropagating}. Goal drift has also been documented over long agentic episodes \citep{arike2025goaldraft} and across inherited contexts \citep{menon2026inherited}. Our setting removes task pressure, reward and memory, isolating reflection on stated values.

% P4: oversight evaluations and measurement.
\paragraph{Oversight evaluations and measurement.}
Behavioural evaluations of corrigibility test shutdown resistance \citep{schlatter2025shutdown}, alignment faking \citep{greenblatt2024alignmentfaking}, in-context scheming \citep{meinke2024scheming} and corrigibility failures in computer-use agents \citep{tien2026rogue}. Methodologically, we draw on discrete-time survival analysis, which has recently been applied to multi-turn consistency in language-model agents \citep{bogdanov2026survival}, and on concerns about the reliability and validity of LLM judges \citep{norman2026reliability,mukherjee2026geometry}, which motivate our two-judge design.
